🌏 Setting the Stage

The Philippine Revolution against Spain began in 1896. In 1897 the revolutionary leader Emilio Aguinaldo went into exile in Hong Kong. Then in April 1898 the Spanish-American War broke out, and on May 1, 1898 the American fleet destroyed the Spanish fleet in the Battle of Manila Bay.

With Spain weakened, Aguinaldo came back to the Philippines on May 19, 1898, aboard the American ship USS McCulloch. He quickly rebuilt the revolutionary army, and on May 25 he set up his military headquarters at Teatro Caviteño in Cavite. The stage was set for a major battle.


⚔️ The Battle of Alapan at a Glance
📅 DateMay 28, 1898
📍 PlaceBarrio Alapan, Imus, Cavite
🇵🇭 Filipino sideRevolutionary army led by Emilio Aguinaldo
🇪🇸 Spanish sideA garrison of roughly 270 to 300 troops under General Leopoldo García Peña
⏰ DurationAbout five hours, from around 10:00 AM to 3:00 PM
🏆 ResultFilipino victory; the Spanish troops surrendered
🔗 ImportanceFirst major victory of Aguinaldo's renewed campaign, and the first time the national flag was unfurled in victory

🕰️ How the Day Unfolded
1. MorningAguinaldo's forces moved against Spanish positions scattered across Cavite and met the garrison at Alapan.
2. FightingThe battle raged for about five hours. The Spanish defenders ran low on ammunition.
3. SurrenderOverwhelmed by the combined Filipino forces, the Spanish troops gave up.
4. March to Cavite PuertoAguinaldo led about 300 Spanish prisoners, including General García Peña, to Cavite Puerto (now Cavite City).
5. The flagThere Aguinaldo unfurled the new flag he had ordered made in Hong Kong.
6. Days afterBy May 31, 1898, the rest of Cavite had been freed from Spanish control.

🚩 The Flag
✏️ Designed byEmilio Aguinaldo, while in exile in Hong Kong
🧵 Sewn byMarcela Agoncillo, her daughter Lorenza Agoncillo, and Delfina Herbosa de Natividad
🎨 ColorsBlue, red, and white
⭐ SymbolsA white triangle, three stars, and a sun with eight rays. The three stars are often said to stand for Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao. The eight rays are commonly said to stand for the first eight provinces that rose up against Spain.
1️⃣ First unfurledMay 28, 1898, after the Battle of Alapan, at Cavite Puerto
2️⃣ Unfurled againJune 12, 1898, at Aguinaldo's home in Kawit, during the proclamation of Philippine independence

🎌 National Flag Days

The Philippines remembers the flag every year from May 28 to June 12. The period starts on the anniversary of the Battle of Alapan and ends on Independence Day, so it ties together the first raising of the flag and the proclamation of independence two weeks later.

The opening ceremonies are held at Imus Heritage Park, and reenactment groups have performed scenes from the revolution on the grounds.


🪧 From Marker to Heritage Park

The site was first honored with a marker and a monument. In a document written in 1948, Aguinaldo himself described the monument at Alapan: a woman standing for the Filipino people, holding a flag in one hand and a broken chain in the other, standing on three mountains marked with three stars.

The site was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1993. Later, the city government built a bigger and more accessible memorial. Its project was approved in March 2009, and the park was inaugurated on May 28, 2014, the anniversary of the battle.

Sources differ on the park's size: early accounts of the 2014 park mention about 16,000 square meters, while newer ones list about 5 hectares.

🏘️ More Imus History

Imus was once part of Cavite el Viejo (now Kawit), together with Cavite City and Noveleta. It gained its own Recollect parish, which gave the people religious independence from the older Jesuit parish. Later on, Bacoor was separated from Imus in 1906, and Dasmariñas followed in 1917.

Besides Alapan, Imus was the site of another famous fight, the Battle of Imus on September 3, 1896. Together these two Katipunan victories are why the city is so closely tied to the Philippine Revolution.
